[Current Understanding, JU response]
JU: The 70-80% value seems outdated. Interval metering exists on more than 70% of distribution circuits in New York State. The JU request clarity on the source of this data.
[Current Understanding, paragraph 2]
In a scenario where minimum day time load (MDL) data is not available the utilities determine it based on the assumption that minimum day time load is 15%-40% of peak load. The assumption varies across each utility, “Technical Guidance Matrix for Integrating DER - September 2022” documents the different assumptions.

[O&R note, JU response]
JU: For O&R, the 8 AM – 3 PM window was updated to 8 AM – 8 PM in 2020.
[Questions, JU answer 1]
JU: This assumption does not apply for all companies. Some companies may use the measured feeder minimum loading values and DER output during the DER’s operational window, or not use the minimum load at all for thermal analyses.
[Questions, JU answer 2]
JU: The JU will use measured data from field devices, where available, instead of the assumptions. Where field data is unavailable, minimum load values are based on extrapolation techniques and engineering assumptions based on data from area station and local telemetry values. Some utilities may not use the minimum loading values for the station thermal screen.
