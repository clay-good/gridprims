# 16: Data quality and continuity audit (is anyone auditing US public energy data?)

Checked 2026-10-29. **Verified** means a primary or authoritative source was opened today (agency page, Federal Register text, journal article, repository record) and the number read off it. **Unverified** means trade press, advocacy secondary, a search-index extract, or a page that would not open. Research for sections 3, 4 and 6 was run by parallel agents; the session's web-search budget (200 calls) ran out before a handful of items could be closed, and those are marked.

## Bottom line

- **Nobody audits these datasets systematically, and the one body chartered to do it has gone quiet.** EIA's own external reviewer, the **ASA Committee on Energy Statistics**, has a public meeting archive that stops around 2013-2017 (*Unverified* that it still convenes). EIA has **no "changes to data" page at all** — `eia.gov/about/changes/` is a 404 — so the only machine-readable signal of survey change is the Federal Register API (**25 EIA information-collection notices since 2025-01-01**). *Verified.*
- **Quality work exists but is all private plumbing, never a published audit.** PUDL runs hundreds of validation tests (migrating to dbt), DOIs every raw input version, and emits run-summary JSON — but publishes **no user-facing quality or continuity report**. Open Grid Emissions buries cleaning changes in release notes. Electricity Maps and WattTime document validation; neither publishes a report. *Verified.*
- **EIA-930's problems are quantified in the literature, not by EIA.** Ruggles et al. (2020), on 54 BAs over 2015-2019: **2.16% of hours missing (41,015)**, **2.64% flagged for imputation (50,031 of 1,893,456)**; OVEC missing **34%** of hours with **16%** of values identical to the prior hour; SEC **13%** negative-or-zero. PUDL's own re-imputation reports **2-3% MAPE** at BA level. EIA acknowledges the EIA-930 vs EIA-923 gap in *Today in Energy* (2025-05-27) **without a single number**. *Verified.*
- **The 2025-2026 continuity shock is real, severe, and unledgered for energy.** ASA (2025-11-21) rates EIA resources **WEAK**: **~35% staffing loss since FY24**, FY26 request flat at **$135M / 246 staff**. EPA **eGRID is frozen at eGRID2023** and the "next release: eGRID2024, January 2026" promise was deleted from the page. **`nrel.gov` no longer resolves** (renamed National Laboratory of the Rockies, 2025-12-01); `afdc.energy.gov` returns 404. NOAA retired Billion-Dollar Disasters **2025-05-08**. *Verified.*
- **The gap is status, not bytes.** Archives (PUDL/Zenodo, Harvard LIL's **311,000-dataset** data.gov mirror, End of Term) preserve files. Trackers (Silencing Science, Data Rescue Project, ASA) record political actions or agency health. **Nothing records, per energy dataset: expected cadence, last actual vintage, posted vs. inferred lateness, the authoritative notice, URL liveness, and archive DOI.** Of 10 visible Silencing Science entries, zero touch energy datasets; Data Rescue Project explicitly declines to quantify removals. *Verified.*
- **The credible model is the FDAAA TrialsTracker plus OpenPrescribing, not an index.** Conservative rules that under-claim violations, deciles instead of ranks, "measures rather than indicators," open code, a correction inbox, and framing the output as leverage the agency can use for its own budget request. The failure mode to avoid is Transparency International's CPI: a single composite score that governments then weaponize. *Verified.*

**Design rule:** every metric above is either a *diff between two things EIA published* or a *check of an identity EIA itself asserts*. Nothing requires us to assert what the true value is. That is the whole credibility trick, and it is the same one the FDAAA TrialsTracker used.

## 6. Tone and risk

**The transferable trick, from the clinical-trials precedent.** FDAAA TrialsTracker worked because the sponsor was already legally responsible for its own registry record, so the monitor never arbitrated truth — "fix your record" was always the cheapest response. Baseline (*Lancet* 395:361-69, 2020-01-17): **1,722 of 4,209 due trials = 40.9% reported on time**; today, live, **25,356 of 32,169 = 78.8%**, notional fines **$130.4B**, fines actually claimed **$0**. Named behavior change: Johns Hopkins staff found NCT02018562 overdue via the tracker and filed the same evening. *Verified.*

**Features that kept it respectful, all adoptable here.** A deliberately **conservative rule that under-claims violations**; a **30-day grace** before anything is called overdue; cases **dropped entirely** when a withheld field would decide applicability, "to avoid ever incorrectly asserting that a trial is in breach"; late-but-fixed items **not flagged forever**; open code, open data, open API; a **correction inbox** in the FAQ. Goldacre's framing: *"I'm not interested in naming and shaming people in order to criticise them… to nudge institutions to prioritise trial reporting."* *Verified.*

**OpenPrescribing supplies the vocabulary.** Every measure page says *"This is indicative only, and should be approached with caution"*, shows **deciles of all peers rather than a rank**, and states: *"These are measures rather than indicators… There may be good reasons."* Measure definitions are open JSON carrying `why_it_matters`, numerator/denominator SQL, `authored_by`, `checked_by`, `date_reviewed`, `next_review`; measures are **retired** when no longer useful. ~20,000 unique users/month per NHSBSA — which also **now funds it**, and credits Bennett's feedback with moving its key dataset from a login portal to a no-login API. *Verified.* That is the outcome to aim at.

**The best single answer to "you're just an adversary": Berkeley Earth.** An independently (and partly Koch-) funded recomputation that **matched** NOAA and NASA, and is now one of six datasets WMO combines for its headline global temperature. Recomputing and agreeing is the most credible thing a monitor can do. *Verified.*

**Three further borrowings.** The **European Statistics Code of Practice peer review** sequence — report shown to the agency, agency approves, **report and the agency's own improvement plan published together**. **PubPeer's** rules: polite, neutral, factual; only publicly verifiable information; **no speculation about motive**; a permanent right of reply. **GLEIF's** data-quality reports: maturity levels and "Top 5 Failing Checks," headlined *"Building trust with transparent quality reports"* — **checks, not rankings**. And **DataCite/Zenodo versioning** gives the way to say "the data changed" without a scandal: immutable snapshot, mutable annotation layer, stable concept identifier, `IsNewVersionOf` links. *Verified.*

**What not to do.** Transparency International now has to **warn governments against weaponizing its own CPI**, and the Maldives' anti-corruption commission dismissed its score by noting only three sources determined it — a single composite number hands critics a methodology fight and hands governments a cudgel. **FAIR assessment tools** show the same failure quantitatively: F-UJI correlates only **ρ = 0.42** with a consensus score, and binary tests penalize partial compliance. **GBD vs WHO** became what the *Lancet* editor called "a Cold War" (malaria 2010: IHME 1,238,000 vs WHO 655,000 deaths) and only ended with MoUs in 2015 and May 2018 committing to a joint study. **Do not ship a score, a rank, or a grade.** *Verified.*

**Real risks, in order.**
1. **The producer gets attacked, not the monitor.** BLS Commissioner Erika McEntarfer was removed **2025-08-01** immediately after a report with a combined **−258,000** revision, the data called "rigged" with no evidence. An audit that quantifies revisions can be strip-mined for exactly that narrative. Mitigation: always frame revision magnitude as **normal statistical behavior with a measured distribution**, and publish the distribution so a single month cannot be cherry-picked.
2. **Advisory channels get dissolved.** Commerce terminated FESAC, the BEA Advisory Committee and three Census committees effective **2025-02-28** (FR notice 2025-06-10); ousted members reconstituted independently and first met **2025-09-18**. Being outside the tent is now the normal state, not a provocation.
3. **Funding collapse is the likeliest death.** FDAAA TrialsTracker was **down or stale ~13 months** (Feb 2024 → Mar 2025) when its grant ended and the ClinicalTrials.gov modernization broke its scraper. A dormant audit is worse than none. Mitigation: keep it a byproduct of snapshots we take anyway, fully static, no live service to fund.
4. **Legal and access risk, bounded.** Gino's $25M suit against Data Colada was dismissed 2024-09-11, but the defense cost **$378,871 from 3,462 donors** and fee-shifting was denied in July 2025. Facebook cut off NYU's Ad Observatory on **2021-08-03**. Mitigation: publish only diffs of public files and identity checks the agency itself asserts; never allege intent.
5. **Precedent for private-first.** Data Colada held its Gino concerns ~20 months before publishing. Adopt a **notify-first window** (we found X, here is the reproducible diff, we publish in N days) and publish the agency's unedited reply inline.

## What to publish (shortlist, in order)

1. **A dated continuity ledger as JSON + a page**, one row per dataset: cadence, last vintage, posted next-release, days late, authoritative notice (FR cite / SCN number / page status string), canonical URL, HTTP status, archive DOI. This is the genuinely unfilled gap and the cheapest for us.
2. **A snapshot diff log** — files added/removed/changed, column changes, row-count deltas, checksums — straight out of what we already compute. Nobody publishes this for energy.
3. **The EIA-930 interchange residual**, per pair and per BA, in MWh and %, plus sum-of-pairs vs reported total net interchange. A single number nobody has ever published for an identity EIA itself asserts.
4. **A revision-magnitude series** for EIA-860/861/923 overlapping periods: how often and by how much published history moves, as a distribution.
5. **A link-liveness report** over every URL we cite, with the Pew 21% baseline for context.
6. **A reliability-coverage table**: who reports SAIDI/SAIFI, under which method, who is on EIA-861S and therefore absent, which years are missing.
7. **Defer**: eGRID vs CAMPD vs EIA-923 reconciliation (needs the unmaintained crosswalk; the bounded version is still worth a one-off note citing Ackerman & Sundquist as the only prior benchmark).

Framing to use, from the precedents: **"checks, not rankings"**; "these are measures rather than indicators"; "this is indicative only"; and offer the result as something an agency can cite in its own budget request — EIA at 35% staffing loss and a flat $135M needs external evidence of strain more than it needs defending.
